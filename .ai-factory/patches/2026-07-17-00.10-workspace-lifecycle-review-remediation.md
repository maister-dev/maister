# Workspace lifecycle review remediation

## Finding

The reviewed lifecycle implementation still had three unsafe seams: the
generic crashed-run discard route bypassed the preserve-first coordinator,
`system_sweep` could outlive its scheduler lease and persist a stale result,
and reconciliation reused a sweep-start timestamp after a finding lease had
expired. The scheduler also reported service-level bundle failures as success.
The generated reconciliation ledger lacked two promised database invariants,
and the API, analytics, ERD, roadmap, and migration-integrity checks still
described the earlier ADR-140/0107-only state.

## Resolution

- Route discard through the shared claimed preserve/remove/finalize coordinator
  and retain `discard` as the durable removal kind.
- Renew and fence the scheduler attempt and job lease throughout a system
  sweep; ignore stale start/result writes and persist bundle failures as
  `SYSTEM_SWEEP_FAILED` attempts while retaining candidate-level retries.
- Pass a fresh clock through reconciliation mutations and preserve a finding
  when its claimed lease is no longer valid.
- Add generated `0108_new_maggott` checks for non-negative attempt counts and
  the resolved-state/timestamp pairing, with real-Postgres coverage.
- Correct ADR-142 API, database, analytics, ERD, roadmap, and migration
  integrity contracts.

## Prevention

For every renewable lease, test both long-running renewal and stale completion
after expiry. Generated follow-up migrations must update the journal test to
locate the owning migration by contract rather than assuming it stays newest.
Treat scheduler service failure and per-candidate retry as distinct observable
outcomes in both the persistence contract and operator documentation.
