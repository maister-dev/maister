# Target actual adapters in native permission fault fixtures

**Date:** 2026-11-04 01:30
**Files:** web/lib/flows/graph/**tests**/permission-result-failure.integration.test.ts; web/lib/flows/graph/**tests**/permission-resume.integration.test.ts; web/lib/flows/graph/**tests**/prompt-owners.integration.test.ts; web/lib/**tests**/permission-crash-boundary.integration.test.ts
**Severity:** medium

## Problem

The full Web integration lane reached neither selected journal publication nor
several intended adapter-crash boundaries. Later permission-resume cases also
encountered retained execution capacity. Crash-boundary teardown could abandon
its database when an unreached proxy barrier correctly failed its drain check.

## Root Cause

Native session ownership now uses a detached gate process that owns the adapter
group. The host's `pid` identifies that gate; `adapterPid` identifies the ACP
adapter. Older fixtures either signaled `pid`, watched the gate for an adapter
SIGSTOP, or selected only direct children in the Supervisor's process group.
Those operations no longer injected the fault named by the test.

The retained publication barrier recorded adapter PID 59712 while the create
receipt and durable obligation identified gate/process group 59711. The barrier
assertion waited for the gate to stop, although the adapter fixture stops itself.
The lane evidence is retained in
`/private/tmp/maister-web-full-integration-final.log` and its sanitized failure
records. Capacity cascades require whole-file reruns; their disappearance is not
assumed from changing the signal target.

## Solution

Use the shared `captureOwnedAdapterProcess` fixture contract before behavioral
signals. It verifies the exact session/run, available assignment and ACP
identity, scoped host ownership attestation, native adapter-to-gate-to-Supervisor
ancestry, invocation ownership and registered process group. Signal the captured
adapter through the checked invocation primitive; never substitute the gate PID.
The journal control retains stopped-state, kernel identity, receipt PID and
atomic-publication assertions. Prompt-owner faults remain run-scoped and retain
the responsive foreign-adapter witness. Manager-driver kills retain their
original targets.

Crash-boundary teardown releases blocked wire operations before joining their
owners, attempts all resource releases and preserves all failures in an
AggregateError, including required-barrier and unfinished-driver assertions.

The subsequent complete native cohort exposed a separate N1 cleanup defect:
three cases failed in `stopRemainingRunAdapters` because its inventory reader
called `executionFor`, which correctly refuses released assignments. Those
cleanup exceptions masked the primary assertions. The fourth N1 interleaving
timed out; its original timeout has no separately established cause. Evidence is retained in
`/private/tmp/maister-native-all-corrected-failures.json`.

N1 inventory now uses the known local host's attested `listSessionsForRun`
read. It acquires no placement and remains available after legitimate release;
the exact native adapter capture is still required before signaling. Both N1
cleanup paths preserve a primary assertion together with any cleanup errors and
attempt every release. After this correction the exact `N1:` selection passed
all five cases, including all three death interleavings, the negative task
failure and the held crash frame: 5 passed, 61 filtered, 26.33 seconds, exit 0.
The previously timed-out interleaving completed in 6.40 seconds; that successful
rerun does not reconstruct its earlier timeout. Scoped ESLint and formatting
pass. Runtime evidence is in `/private/tmp/maister-n1-selected.log` and
`/private/tmp/maister-n1-selected-result.json`. The exact invocation
`46f26a1a-7c11-4fbb-a1d8-146929b4d254` was released through `releaseInvocation`
after natural exit with no errors, recorded in
`/private/tmp/maister-n1-selected-cleanup.json`. The 61 filtered cases are not
qualified by this focused run; complete-suite qualification remains required.

## Prevention

Treat host process metadata according to its explicit lifetime: gate/group
ownership is cleanup evidence, whereas adapter PID is a behavioral fault target.
Never use `adapterPid ?? pid` or match every process with the same executable.
Require positive native ownership before signals and fail when proof is absent.
After fixing an early retained owner, rerun the complete affected matrix to
distinguish capacity cascades from independent failures. Teardown failures must
not skip database or process cleanup, and must remain visible.
