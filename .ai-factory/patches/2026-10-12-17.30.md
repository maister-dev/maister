## Solution

The native selected mode accepts a nonempty explicit PID list, deduplicates it
and reads each PID directly without a global catalogue. The internal plural
API restores requested order/duplicates and gone-peer nulls. Each selected PID
retains kernel PID/UID/start checks and invocation-environment proof; unknown
live kernel identity still raises an explicit ownership error. Observable
foreign/inspector identities grant no signal authority. A known revalidated
kernel identity with unreadable environment remains useful for liveness with
`owned:false`/`inspected:false`; registration and signaling still reject it.
The watchdog compares the original PID/UID/kernel-start identities; PGID is
fresh metadata/group state, not the `sameProcess` equality predicate.
Single-PID loading, snapshots, group retirement and foreign-signal refusal keep
their previous contracts. No stricter shared precondition or cached authority
is introduced.

Use the existing 500ms loop to log the first successful check and every 120
successful checks: selected PIDs, cumulative/maximum lookup wall time, elapsed
span, runtime/platform, invocation/PID/kernel birth and case/role. These are
bounded, overlapping, right-censored windows, not CPU-share or whole-lifetime
totals. Existing diagnostic artifacts retain them. Linux invokes its unchanged
per-PID `/proc` reader through the new plural composition; local Linux
O-identity/parent-death/runner regression remains required separately.

Product APIs/HTTP/SSE/objects, PostgreSQL schemas/SQL/journals, configuration,
deployment and hosting remain unchanged. No migration or ADR allocation is
needed. Both exact Nodes and two web shards each, serial unsharded isolation,
seven owners/50 cases, separate preflight, 60min job and original
