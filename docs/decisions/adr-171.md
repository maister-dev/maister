# ADR-171: Desk home information architecture and the member default route

> Part of the ADR log — hub, index, and editing rules: [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md).

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** `/` renders the project portfolio (`web/app/(app)/page.tsx`,
`PortfolioPage`) — a grid of project cards. That is the right home for an operator
registering repositories and the wrong home for anyone whose question is "what
needs me today". The portfolio answers *which projects exist*; a team member
arriving in the morning is asking *what is blocked on me, what moved, and what is
in flight* — questions that span projects and are not answerable from a card grid.

Two facts make the move cheap. `web/app/(app)/projects/` already exists with
`[slug]/` and `new/` but has **no `page.tsx`**, so `/projects` currently 404s —
the portfolio moves into an empty slot rather than displacing anything. And the
rail is small enough to re-cut in one pass: `RAIL_SECTION_IDS` holds nine ids
(`projects, inbox, studio, observatory, agents, mcps, users, scheduler,
settings`) with no `home`, `work` or `activity` member.

The hazard is that `/` is currently overloaded. `railSectionForPathname`
(`web/components/chrome/left-rail-route.ts`) collapses **four** distinct prefixes
onto one section — `/`, `/projects`, `/runs`, `/scratch-runs` all highlight
`projects` — and seven call sites navigate to `/`, some meaning "home" and at
least one meaning "the portfolio". Those two meanings are about to diverge.

**Decision:** `/` becomes the **Desk**; the portfolio moves to `/projects`
unchanged; the rail gains explicit `home`, `work` and `activity` sections; and a
non-admin's default landing route is `/work`.

**D1 — `/` renders the Desk.** The Desk composes surfaces this milestone builds
elsewhere — Now tiles, the decision queue, work in flight, the activity feed, the
digest sentence — and **composes only**. It re-implements none of them: a second
copy of the decision queue that drifts from `/inbox`'s is exactly the failure this
IA is meant to prevent.

**D2 — The portfolio moves to `/projects`, behaviour unchanged.**
`web/app/(app)/page.tsx` becomes `web/app/(app)/projects/page.tsx` and its
`@/components/portfolio/*` tree moves with it. The onboarding checklist, the empty
state and the new-project tile move as-is. This is a relocation, not a redesign;
anything that renders differently afterwards is a bug in the move.

**D3 — Inbound `/` links are audited individually, because `/` now has two
meanings.** Every call site is classified before the move, not after:

| Call site | Intent | Target after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `web/app/(auth)/layout.tsx:29` (logo) | home | `/` |
| `web/components/chrome/top-nav.tsx:47` (logo) | home | `/` |
| `web/components/feedback/error-fallback.tsx:52` | home | `/` |
| `web/app/change-password/page.tsx:17` | home | `/` |
| `web/app/change-password/actions.ts:68` | home | `/` |
| `web/components/projects/new-project-form.tsx:202` | **the portfolio** | **`/projects`** |
| `web/components/chrome/left-rail-sections.ts` — `projects` section `href` | **the portfolio** | **`/projects`** |

The last two are the reason this is a decision rather than a rename. Leaving
either on `/` would send a user who just created a project, or who clicked
"Projects" in the rail, to the Desk — plausible-looking, silently wrong, and
invisible to any test that only asserts a 200.

**D4 — The rail is re-cut, and `railSectionForPathname` stops collapsing.** The
rail becomes **Home / Projects / Work / Activity / Inbox / Flow Studio /
Observatory**, plus the existing admin-only tail (Agents, MCPs, Users, Scheduler,
Settings). `railSectionForPathname` must be **total over the app's route
prefixes** and must map `/` to `home` — not to `projects`. `/runs` and
`/scratch-runs` continue to highlight `projects`; that collapse is intentional and
survives.

**D5 — A non-admin lands on `/work`; an admin lands on the Desk.** This is a
clause **of this decision**, not a separate routing rule, so that `/` never forks
by role twice — once in the router and again inside the page. "Non-admin" means
`role !== "admin"`, which covers both `member` and `viewer`
(`users.role` is `admin | member | viewer`).

**D6 — Nav visibility is not the authorization boundary.** The rail already hides
admin sections from non-admins (`buildLeftRailSections` appends them only when
`userRole === "admin"`). That is a convenience, not a control: every route the
rail hides is refused server-side on its own, and a member typing an admin URL is
refused regardless of what the rail rendered. Adding sections to the rail changes
no authorization, and no authorization decision may start depending on the rail.

**Consequences:**

- `/projects` stops being a 404 and becomes a real, linkable route — the portfolio
  gains a URL it never had.
- Two navigation targets now exist where one did, so every future "go home" needs
  to answer *which* home. D3's table is the standing answer.
- Deep links to `/` that predate this change land on the Desk. For a logo click
  that is correct; the one case where it would have been wrong is D3's
  post-creation redirect, which moves.
- The rail grows from a four-item core to seven. That is close to the point where
  a rail needs grouping; this ADR does not introduce grouping and notes the
  pressure.
- Admins and non-admins now have different first screens, so "what do you see when
  you sign in" has two answers in support conversations.

**Alternatives Considered:**

- **Keep the portfolio on `/` and put the Desk at `/desk`.** Rejected: the home
  route is the one a reader reaches by reflex, and giving it to the surface that
  answers "what needs me" is the entire point. A Desk nobody navigates to is a
  page, not a home.
- **Render the Desk for everyone, including non-admins.** Rejected per D5 — a
  member's Desk is mostly the work table anyway, and sending them straight to the
  full table skips a hop.
- **Fork inside `page.tsx` by role instead of routing.** Rejected: `/` would then
  mean two different pages at one URL, which breaks deep-linking, caching, and the
  rail's active-section calculation simultaneously.
- **Redirect `/` to `/projects` and leave every call site alone.** Rejected: it
  preserves the ambiguity D3 exists to remove, and costs a redirect on the most
  visited route.
