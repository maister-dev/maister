# ADR-174: The Desk renders one object per work item

> Part of the ADR log — hub, index, and editing rules: [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md).

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** [ADR-172](adr-172.md) D1 made `/` the Desk and settled that it
**composes** — Now tiles, the decision queue, work in flight, the activity feed and
the digest sentence, each rendered through the component its owning surface renders.
That decision prevented the failure it named: no region drifted, because no region
was reimplemented.

It did not prevent a different one. Composition says nothing about whether two
composed regions describe the *same* thing, and on the Desk three of them do.

The finding is counted, not impressionistic. On one screen a single task was
observed **four times** — in the rail's active workspaces, as a HITL card, as a
promotable card, and as a work-table row — and a second task the same four times
plus six activity rows. Two tasks, roughly sixteen objects.

The duplication is structural rather than cosmetic.
[`STAGE_BY_KIND`](../../web/lib/queries/decisions.ts) maps three of the four decision
populations onto stages that are members of `WORK_IN_FLIGHT_STAGES`
([`web/lib/work/stage.ts`](../../web/lib/work/stage.ts)):

| Decision kind | Stage | In `WORK_IN_FLIGHT_STAGES`? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hitl` | `WaitingOnHuman` | yes |
| `crashed` | `Crashed` | yes |
| `promotable` | `Review` | yes |
| `flagged` | `Held` | **no** — `WORK_BACKLOG_STAGES` |

For three populations out of four, the Decisions region and the work table are two
renderings of one population. Reordering the page moves the copies closer together;
only merging them removes the duplication.

A second, independent duplication sits in the header: the digest sentence and the Now
tiles are the same five numbers rendered as words and then as digits, adjacent — by
construction, as [`web/lib/queries/digest.ts`](../../web/lib/queries/digest.ts)
documents in its own comments.

**Decision:** the Desk renders **one object per work item**. The work table is the
spine; a decision is a property of a row, not a region beside it.

**D1 — The work table, grouped by project, is the spine of the page.** Every other
region is either a summary of it (the strip) or a different kind of fact about it
(activity). This is what makes "one object per work item" checkable: there is one
place a work item is allowed to appear.

**D2 — Duplication is resolved by merging the decision into the row, not by splitting
the populations across regions.** The alternative — keeping both regions and
partitioning which decision kinds each may render — preserves two renderings and adds
a rule about which one wins. A row that carries its own decision has no such rule.
`Held` is the exception that proves it: it is the one decision kind
`WORK_IN_FLIGHT_STAGES` does not carry, so it keeps a region of its own.

**D3 — The number strip is current state only — no period, no selector, no window.**
The Desk answers "what is true now". A windowed number ("12 promoted this week")
answers a different question, needs a period control to be legible, and invites a
second control to change it. The digest read models survive for the
[ADR-173](../decisions.md#adr-173-user-notification-subscriptions-and-web-push-over-the-widened-outbound-webhook-engine)
notification trigger, where a window is the point; they stop being rendered here.

**D4 — Repeated activity events are not collapsed, grouped or reordered.** Chronology
is the feed's contract. Collapsing "5 runs crashed" into one row is a summary, and
summaries belong in the strip; the feed's value is that it preserves what happened in
the order it happened.

**D5 — `/work` gains expandable rows in a later increment.** The row component is
shared between the Desk and `/work` and must not be forked (ADR-172 amendment 2), so
expansion ships behind a prop defaulting to off. Turning it on for `/work` — and
deciding what a backlog or settled row expands into, which the Desk never has to
answer — is its own piece of work.

**Supersession.** This supersedes [ADR-172](adr-172.md) **D1 in part**. D1 enumerates
the composed regions *as a decision*; this removes two of them (the digest sentence
and the Decisions region) and inverts the remaining order. D1's principle — the Desk
composes and never reimplements — is **reaffirmed, not replaced**: the merged row
still renders through the owning surface's components, and D2 of this ADR extends the
principle to the HITL panel by requiring `/inbox` to be rebuilt on the extracted
panel rather than left as a second copy.

**Consequences:**

- The Desk adds no mutation path and no query. The row→decision join is built in the
  page from the decision queue it already loads, keyed on `runId`; `getWorkTable` is
  untouched. A read-model change would have been the tell that the merge was really a
  rewrite.
- A reader who wants the decision queue as a queue goes to `/inbox`, which is
  unchanged. The Desk stops being a second inbox.
- `HitlCard` is split so that `/inbox` and the Desk render one panel implementation.
  This is the ADR-172 D1 principle applied to a component that was monolithic before
  anything needed to share it.
- The strip's five tiles are the five `WORK_IN_FLIGHT_STAGES` members, so the strip
  and the table are one population by construction rather than by agreement. Their
  sum is total work in flight, which is deliberately **not** the number of visible
  rows — the table slices.
- Two orphan message keys (`desk.sub`, `desk.nowLabel`) are exposed by the key-coverage
  assertion this change adds. They predate it; the assertion is what makes them
  fixable.
- The tokens tile disappears from the Desk while the ADR-173 push payload still
  prints its clause. `queryTokensSpentSince` measures the lifetime cost of runs merely
  *started* in the window and `run_cost_rollups` has no time dimension, so a truthful
  per-period figure is not computable from it today. That is tracked separately and
  must not be closed by deleting the clause.

**Alternatives Considered:**

- **Reorder the page and keep both regions.** Rejected: adjacency makes the
  duplication easier to see, not smaller. The counted example had the same task in
  four places regardless of their order.
- **Partition the decision kinds — Decisions renders `hitl`, the table renders the
  rest.** Rejected per D2: it keeps two renderings of one population and adds a
  precedence rule to remember, which is a drift surface of exactly the kind ADR-172 D1
  exists to close.
- **Drop the work table and keep a richer Decisions region.** Rejected: the decision
  queue covers only work that needs a human *now*. Work in flight that needs nobody
  would vanish from the home screen, and "what is running" is half the question the
  Desk exists to answer.
- **Fork the row component so the Desk can expand and `/work` cannot.** Rejected:
  ADR-172 amendment 2 made `WorkTableLabels extends WorkRowsLabels` precisely so a
  column change is a compile error at both call sites. A fork trades that guarantee
  for a prop default, which D5 gets anyway.
- **Keep the digest sentence and drop the tiles.** Rejected: the tiles are
  navigable — a tile filters the table in place — and a sentence is not. Of the two
  renderings of the same five numbers, the interactive one is the one worth keeping.
